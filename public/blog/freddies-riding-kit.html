I work for the distributor of a few brands in the UK – so the kit I ride is dictated by that – let’s get that out of the way straight off the bat. Thankfully they are brands who have tended to circulate the top of the foiling food chain and they produce some great kit. Take my views with a pinch of salt – I am a sales rep after all – but I ride this gear and these specific brands a lot and have done for 7 years now, so if you’re interested in any of my views on said products and brands, here they are.

The aim of this blog is to give you a run through of what kit I’m riding at this moment in time – Autumn 2026. I’m trying to persuade Liam to do the same with his own personal kit and thoughts, to give you guys an idea of what we ride and why.

As a caveat – I’m 35 years old, probably classed as semi-advanced wing foiling now, comfortable in decent sized swell and I’m 82kg stood bollock naked on the scales.

Mine and Liam’s riding centres around waves and bumps of any sorts. 80% of what we do is now parawing and wing in waves – this dictates a lot about the kit we choose to ride.

Freddie’s current kit at a glance:

  • AXIS Surge (740, 830, 890, 1010), Ultrashort fuse, 320 Surf Skinny, 82cm UHM mast
  • AXIS Mini Dart 70L, AXIS Mako 110L, Appletree Appleslice 32L
  • Ozone PRV2 3m and 3.6m, Flow V2 4.3m

Foils

AXIS, always. Ridden since the beginning – we’ve gone 1020 Original surf foil, through to Pump n Glides, BSC, HPS, ART V1, Spitfires, ART v2’s and finally the Surges. Maybe I need to write a blog post about the journey through the whole AXIS range down the line, but it’s far too long to go in here.

In the AXIS range for 2025/2026 my riding has been dominated by the Surge – an AR10 Surf foil. At AR10 this probably has a little more glide than a downright surf foil and so has handled winging and parawinging equally well – I don’t think parawinging on the likes of the Spitfire for instance would’ve been as much fun as the turn to glide ratio just isn’t the same as the Surge’s and I would’ve had to ride something bigger than I do on the Surges. 12 months down the line of riding these foils and I’m still excited about going out on them.

The 890 is the go-to size for me. It’s my favourite foil in the bulk of conditions we get and can handle up to shoulder high waves if it has to. Nowadays so much of our time is spent parawinging in less critical conditions – think about Hood River, it can get huge, but it never breaks. Our home spot in Exmouth is the same. There’s less power, less energy in the waves here and the 890 does a great job in these spots. Likewise, because it’s glidey and the Surge pumps well, this has been a great size for my incredibly fledgling prone foiling career. I’d never managed to link waves proning before the Surge came along, and while I’d still class my level as very poor – this foil has been responsible for a lot of progression since it was released.

Once we get overhead the 830 comes out. There’s a significant drop in glide on this size, but given it only comes out on the bigger parawing days when you can carry a tonne of speed from bump to bump, or on more wing days when glide is less of an issue – it’s fine.

I’ve got a 740 on demo for this year which I’ve only used towing once so far – but if we get some decent wing conditions at a more critical beach break (like Gwithian for instance) then I would love to get out winging on this foil.

The 1010 Surge has been a real surprise to me. AXIS has always been known as a brand that makes some of the best bigger foils – for entering downwinding or for the bigger guys. Personally, I’ve never gotten on great with them – they’re so big and numb, and jumping on them only on occasion from the smaller Spitfires and Surges hasn’t given my brain or body enough time to get used to the different characteristics of these larger foils. I’ve used PnG’s, PnG V2’s and Fireballs and while I can acknowledge their need to exist – I don’t enjoy using them.

The 1010 Surge has been the first foil I’ve felt crosses over beautifully between a regular wing foiler who wants to throw their kit around but needs something to handle gliding between smaller bumps, SUP foiling and downwinding. This was a breath of fresh air when I got on a downwind board – no longer struggling to pump and turn the behemoth PnG 1300 or Fireball 1250. Granted, it doesn’t have the downright low end of those two foils – but the usability goes through the roof for someone whose brain was programmed on smaller foils.

Ultrashort Advance+ black fuselage. Don’t bother with anything else, this does it all. This size alone makes up about 90% of our fuselage sales. 5% the same model but in Short.

Surf Skinny rear wings are the go-to nowadays. I jumped on the 300 straight away but I’ve been playing a lot on the 320 more recently to try reducing the pitch sensitivity of the overall setup. The Surges are pitchy – Liam hated it when he had a quick go. AXIS have always generally been pitchy in general, but the Surge is a tad more. I’ve gone back on the 320 to slow that down, and so I can work on my turning technique. Once I’ve gotten this a bit more dialled in the aim is to go back on the 300 as it is noticeably more fun to ride. It’s only when it gets big and you’re going Mach 10 it feels like you’re on a rocking horse sometimes.

The UHM mast is the one, if you have it in your budget. I appreciate they’re not cheap, but the stiffness difference over anything else is awesome. 82cm for everything now.

Boards

A mixture between AXIS and Appletree over the years. 3 main boards – Prone/Tow/Kite, Downwind and Wing/Parawing.

Prone/Tow/Kite is the Appleslice prone from Appletree – 32L. This gets the least amount of use but is a lovely bit of kit when it comes out the bag. My aim is still to get better at prone, but we’ll see – I’ve been saying the same for 5 years. The Appletree construction stiffness is also noticeable – you feel it in your riding.

Downwind SUP has been dominated by the AXIS x Frank Mako for a couple of years now. While we’ve seen interest in SUP foil worldwide slow down since the inception of parawing, this is still such a lovely board that I am reluctant to get rid of anytime soon. We try to circulate demo boards through relatively quickly at work to prevent their value dropping too much – but what’m I gonna replace it with, the same thing? There’s no other options for me. This is the daddy. 110L seemed a little small to begin with, but after a bit of practice and a couple goes on both the 130L and 120L made most sense. Not the easiest thing to balance on at all, but it rockets off the water with a bit of skill. Worth considering sizing though – while I’m by no means accomplished in SUP foil, I’ve had a decent bit of practice in total. This is not an easy board to paddle – and I’ve watched other proficient Wing Foilers struggle to even stand on this thing. It’s a commitment.

Wing/Parawing. This has evolved so much over recent years, but it seems to have settled now to a general midlength shape. I’ve spent the last 10 months or so on an AXIS x Frank Mini Dart 70L, and I love it. Prior to that I was on an Appletree Midlength V1 67L, and I loved that equally. I’m already looking at moving this Mini Dart on to move over to the V2 Midlength from Appletree, purely because I’ve had the Mini Dart for a while. I’m a definite fan of the midlength shape. It might not be overly needed for all out wing-foilers, but I spend 70/80% of my water time on a parawing nowadays where it comes into its own getting going and on touch downs. Both these boards are great. Both their designers are friends and both are great guys producing top quality kit.

I went for 70L as a decent compromise in riding vs. flotation. Body weight volume would be 80/85L but this feels too big for me at my riding ability. 60L would be preferred, and I spent a lot of time winging an Appletree 55L wingboard prior to parawinging, but this discipline has me wanting a little more volume for getting going in slighter winds.

Wings

I sell Ozone and Flysurfer. Flysurfer sell some banging foil kites, but haven’t quite managed to captivate people with their inflatable kites or Parawings yet. This could well change in time, but for now it means I’m almost solely on Ozone for everything – Kitesurf, Wing and Parawing. You lot aren’t here to talk about kitesurfing so we’ll gloss right over that one!

Wings – given the choice, it’s all about the Flow V2 these days – It’s more wave focussed than the Flux and by now you’ve realised this is exactly where my focus is. It’s October 2026 and the Flow V2 Ultra-X has only just rocked up in stock and we’ve yet to get one on demo. I’ve no doubt with the extra stiffness across the main T-Junction on the leading edge this is going to be a banger. Realistically, with how often I wing I’m not as fussed as I once was about the latest and greatest in winging. If it gets me into the waves easy, flags out and I can forget about it while I rip the shit out of a wave (in my head) – I’m happy. And this is what the Flow V2 does – Aluula UX or not.

Size wise, I tend to wing in lighter conditions now – and I’m relatively well decided that a 4.3m is all you need nowadays. There’s a 5m in the van, but it’s unlikely I would buy one for myself if it wasn’t a work demo. I’ve never been much of a fan of anything bigger than 4.3m as they become more of a handful and I don’t really enjoy just mowing the lawn. If it’s light winds, Liam and I tend to kitefoil – or at least we used to. The 4.3m is a great, all round size that still handles like a smaller wing but will get you going in some pretty light conditions.

Parawings – the current ongoing infatuation. Yes, I’m very happy we’ve had the Ozone brand the last couple years since parawinging became a thing. I knew at least one of our kite brands (both with Paraglider heritage) would smash the ball out of the park, and it turned out to be Ozone. The Pocket Rocket V1 was a banger, I enjoyed the innovations that turned up in the Powerpack V1, and I have loved the PRV2 that’s come this year. This suits my needs and does everything it needs to be done. I’ve had a couple of goes on the Afterburner since that’s arrived – and it’s epic, but it’s for a different goal. A goal that I don’t have. The PRV2 is the perfect allrounder – it does everything I need it to do, and it does it easily and comfortably. The bulk of our riding at our home spot involves 3/4/5 tacks upwind and a 1km max downwind run back through the bumps. Even at Daymer down in Cornwall, one tack out across the bay and back and you’re right back at the heart of the swell run. It’s been exciting to watch the progression this year in parawings and I look forward to see where some of the other brands go as well – it sounds like this Frigate V2 is a good bit of kit. I had a go in Hood River this year and it felt equally as great as the PRV2.

Sizes again – 3m is the sweet spot in parawinging I think. It’s such a lovely size to handle and stow away – it’s just a shame it needs so much wind to get it going. I’ve used 4.3m’s in the past but they can be a bit much to handle when it comes to stowing relatively frequently. With a year’s experience under my belt now, it feels like the sweet spot is the 3.6m size. Saying that – the 4.3m Afterburner I used had such a big wind range and still collapsed relatively well, I think I’d look at that if I were to use that model more.

There you go, there’s some thoughts.
