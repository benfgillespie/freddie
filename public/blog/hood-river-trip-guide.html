We’ve been 3 times now, and people keep asking us questions about how to do it. It’s still not a very easy/popular trip to do for our fellow Brits and info is lacking sometimes. It’s surprisingly easy though and definitely something to consider if you’ve got time in or around the UK summer season to get away.

As a caveat, we know very little still – and our trips are a little different to most – We stay in the back of a truck and scrounge off the mates in the industry we’ve made along the way, sleeping on their driveways and eating their food, in return for beers mainly.

Saying that, here’s a very rough guide to a trip in Hood River and a few bits we’ve learnt on our trips that you might enjoy.

The Columbia River Gorge Trip Guide – quick reference

  • Where: Hood River, Oregon – around an hour inland from Portland (PDX), roughly 10½ hours direct from London Heathrow.
  • When: June to August for the warmest, most consistent wind. April/May can be good too if you don’t mind a wetsuit and want fewer people around.
  • Main spot: Spring Creek Hatchery (Washington side) for the best bumps. The Event Site (Oregon side) for easier, flat-water beginner riding.
  • Stay: Hood River (bigger, more going on) or White Salmon (smaller, quieter) – only 10 minutes apart.
  • Eat: Mexican, every night. No arguments.
  • Budget: Car hire is essential, and accommodation and food add up fast – this isn’t a cheap trip.
  • Book: 10 days to 2 weeks gives you enough slack for the inevitable no-wind days.

Where is it and when should I go?

Hood River is a town, about an hour directly inland of Portland, Oregon – the State above California on the west coast of the USA. Roughly 10 ½ hours from London Heathrow, direct. It’s on the very north of Oregon and the river there is the split between Oregon to the south and Washington State to the North. Its popularity in windsports comes from the heavily localised wind that funnels down the Columbia River Gorge. Combined with the river flow in the opposite direction this can create great big bumps that rumble their way down the river in certain areas.

What can I do there?

Obviously we went for the bump riding. Yes, kitesurfing exists here, as does a lot of windsurfing. The windsports scene in general is massive, but we obviously came with relatively specific goals. Our first trip aim in 2024 was to try nail down and tick off competence in SUP Downwind. Supposedly the gorge is a great place to learn SUP downwind – and it is – but that’s not to say it’s still a very easy discipline. We brought kitesurf gear but we didn’t use it. The second year and third year trips have been dominated by Parawinging – and while yes, this is currently the main focus of the industry in general, it is an amazing spot for it – and one we think perfectly suits the discipline. Easy, unbreaking bumps that can rumble on for miles and a pretty easy ability to get back upwind with the current.

Worth mentioning it also sounds like a mecca for mountain biking, hiking and kayaking. If you’re travelling with someone who might not want to spend the entirely possible 8 hours on the water – there’s a tonne of other options. You will notice in the height of summer though, the wind sports folks will be on the water 7 days a week, 30ish days a month as much as possible. By the time we’ve gotten there in September for our trips based around the AWSI event – the locals are usually all entirely burnt out and ready for a break.

When should we go?

Annoyingly, it’s a direct crossover with British summertime. I only say annoyingly as the UK sucks for 4/5 months of the year – I’ve always preferred going away on any holidays in those 4/5 months to break up the winter. British summertime and the shoulder seasons can be awesome, and I always feel it’s a shame to miss them. Anyway – Hood River works the same way. It’s a May through to September spot. The wind and the temperatures are more favourable and routine through these summer months – no doubt down to the temperature being a big factor in how these winds are created.

Good days can be had prior and post these months, but are less dependable. The AWSI event has always historically been held at the start of September, and has always been given grief for that from those who want the best conditions. The Gorge tends to wind down as of the end of August in terms of warm water and consistent conditions. Saying this, our first and second trips here in 2024 and 2025 around the first week of September have had banging conditions the entire time. 2026 though, not so much. Temperatures were considerably colder, we had actual rain and when the wind did come in it was lighter and didn’t produce the bump size we’d gotten used to in the previous years.

To give you some context – 2024 and 2025 we could’ve travelled solely with 3m parawings and 700/800cm2 foils. The idea of using a larger parawing on these two trips would’ve been laughed at – it’s howling. 2026 was more like 3.6m/4m Parawings and 1000cm2 foils. Colder temperatures doesn’t bring in the strong wind and the lighter winds don’t bring in the bump size. Those first couple trips we also spent barely any time in wetsuits – maybe only in the cooler morning sessions before the sun broke through properly. Third year trip was entirely wetsuit riding – granted only 2mm wetsuits.

In conclusion – June/July/August are the warmest and most consistent months for conditions. We hear from the locals that April and May are also good, but you’d definitely wanna be in a wetsuit of some sorts around then as nothing’s warmed up yet. If you’re a Brit who’s used to wearing a wetsuit and wants to ride with less people – these earlier months would suit. Hood River gets busy in the summer.

Where should I ride?

There’s more than one spot – that’s what we hadn’t appreciated. Most of the work goes down at the Spring Creek Hatchery, on the Washington side. This is the most popular spot with both an easy and sketchy entry into some decent bumps and upwind/downwind riding.

The Tunnel carparks up river of the Hatchery were popular for a while with SUP Downwind guys but always involved a very sketchy gravel ‘carpark’ entry/exit and run across the road in full gear. We weren’t overly surprised this year to hear the ‘council’ had closed these ‘carparks’ out of fear of people being mown down by timber lorries. Honestly, I think this has happened at the right time anyway – the Upwind ability in parawings nowadays has left these runs fairly useless, and it always seemed like a bit of a waste of fuel and time to us before….

For more beginner riders, there’s flat water back on the Oregon side near where the Event Site actually is that hosts the AWSI event. Grassy launches, tonnes of parking, tonnes of setup space and flat water entry make this a bit more of a haven for beginner/intermediate riders. You can even spend 15/20 minutes smashing upwind to the Hatchery spot from here still if needs be – it’s all in the same river just further down and creates for a fun little mini adventure. For any kiters – this is where the local guys have their world famous slider park that’s been seen on many a video over the years. There’s a great scene of park rats down here, keeping this dying discipline of kitesurfing alive!

‘Out East’ is something we kept hearing in our first trip that we didn’t understand for a while. For those of you - like us - who have watched the likes of Jack Ho’s videos riding HUGE rolling swell on the gorge, you might be surprised to hear that those spots aren’t actually in Hood River area. They’re an hour down the road, ‘Out East’. Rufus and Arlington are the two we’ve sampled and seen, but it sounds like there’s even more that blow up in the right conditions. We don’t know enough to tell you about these places – but find yourself a local or chat to one of the shops back in town and find out for yourselves if and when they might work. Essentially, it goes bonkers in these spots – but it needs even more wind. Rufus gets INCREDIBLE, but it needs a serious amount of wind for it to be worth it. The rare days we’ve had here in the trips we’ve done have definitely been worth the trip – but at a dollar a mile charge in the Uhaul truck, it can be a bit of a killer when it doesn’t pay off!

The Towns and where to stay

This is where our trips may differ to yours. We’ve always done these trips on a shoe string budget and found ourselves camping out the back of a truck in random places around town. For those of you on a better budget, wanting a more ‘traditional’ holiday/trip – you might want to investigate Airbnbs or hotels. We can’t help you much with that, but they’re everywhere - and they’re expensive. Nothing about this trip is cheap.

Hood River is the bigger town, on the Oregon side of the river. White Salmon is directly across the river from it on the Washington side. There’s more going on in Hood, it’s a bigger spot. More bars, more restaurants, more night life. White Salmon is smaller and quieter but has some great, chilled eateries and bars. The wind sports shops are all in Hood River. Neither town is better. Liam and I are probably more suited personally to White Salmon, but when it comes to the AWSI event and where most folks are – there’s just more going on in Hood. They’re only 10 minutes away from each other – with a very dodgy bridge linking the two that probably wouldn’t pass inspections here in the UK. It’s a couple of quid each time you cross one way, which can rack up over a couple weeks – but not enough to worry about.

The shops

Five spots in town worth knowing if you need gear, repairs or a demo – all in Hood River. Worth mentioning – brands share themselves between each of the shops, so check out which shop sells your favourite bits before you go perhaps.

  • Windance Board Shop: 108 Hwy 35. The OG – windsurf, kite, wing and foil gear.
  • Big Winds: 207 Front Street. Windsurfing, kiteboarding and SUP.
  • Doug’s Hood River: 101 Oak Street. Wing foiling, kiteboarding, stand-up foiling and parawinging, plus apparel – and a separate watersports lesson centre at the Event Site.
  • Pure Stoke Sports: 202 State Street. Wing foiling and kiteboarding among its year-round outdoor sports range.
  • Hydrocraft Systems: 708 Oak Street. Hood River’s own wingfoil brand – foils, boards and travel bags, locally owned and designed, plus accessories like wetsuits and helmets.

Eateries

My favourite. There are some great spots here, and they’re all entirely excessive. I could write 1000 words on each spot, but I’ve decided to refrain from that and just outline the basics.

Morning – plenty to choose from, but the four we’ve spent the bulk of our time frequenting have been: White Salmon Bakery, Pine Street Bakery, Egg River Cafe and Nativ Café. Yes, everyone mentions Bette’s in Hood River. Funnily enough though, both Liam and I decided quickly that this was just a stretch too far for us in terms of Americanism and excessive eating. Sorry readers, but this just wasn’t our cup of tea. They do make an epic breakfast burrito that you can take away though – this is well worth it if you’re flying by to the Hatchery.

Lunch – don’t worry about it, you’re gonna be riding through lunch. Just eat the rest of your leftover breakfast burrito.

Dinners - Mexican, Mexican, Mexican. God damnit, we don’t get food like this at home. Mexican spots in the UK just don’t exist, and if they do – they’re usually a frustrating knock off that must compensate for so many different things because it’s just not available in the UK. We don’t get corn tortillas in the UK, only flour. We only just got Modelo in the UK this year, and the bulk of it is brewed in the midlands here – a sacrilege!

We’ve yet to decide on a favourite Taqueria – Bingen (next to White Salmon) or Hood River. I would lean towards the Hood River one simply for character and for the memories we have there – but the food in the Bingen Taqueria is equally awesome. The Hood River option is so popular and easy, during the AWSI week you’re almost guaranteed to bump into foiling royalty sat at a table there in the evening getting their fix.

There’s of course tonnes of bar eateries in both towns. Notable mentions would be Everybody’s Brewery in White Salmon for beer and burgers, Double Mountain Brewery in HR for beer and pizzas and pFriem brewery in HR for beers and beers. Do you see the theme here?

Lastly on the eateries – there’s a proper Barbeque spot near the Hood River event site that we’ve only eaten at once but was incredible if you like your barbeque. 45 minute waits, a line out the door and only open over the weekends, yet well worth the trip. Grasslands Barbeque.

What else do I need to do?

Rent a car – that’s a given. America doesn’t seem to be an easy place to get around without a vehicle. You can rent them in Portland when you fly in and drive down, or rent one when you arrive in HR and suss out the train/taxi situation to get from Portland. Seems like there’s only one taxi driver in HR – everyone kept suggesting the same guy to us. We used him in the second year when we couldn’t get a lift back to the airport – but we reckon using him there’s a 50% chance you’ll be abducted and forced into an arranged Philippine marriage. Weird chat.

Book a decent amount of time there. 10 days to 2 weeks. Gives you plenty of leeway if there’s no wind some days as it’s tres unlikely you’ll not get anything over a time frame like that. There’s also a bunch of other stuff to do there if you get bored or tired of being on the water 24 hours a day.

  • Rent a mountain bike – electric or not – the trails round the area are awesome and accessible from town.
  • Go hiking – same again, the trails round the area are awesome and accessible from town.
  • Visit the Dam Hatcheries to see the Salmon ladders. There’s a few about – we’ve been into both sides of the Bonneville Fish Hatchery and they were both a good bit of fun.
  • Go fishing. Take a rod, pay for a 3 day pass and go try catch one of these big ass Salmon they’re all pulling out. White Salmon river outlet was a hot spot for a bunch of the foilers to go, early doors and try get something. Ask Liam more info.
  • Just go for a drive and find yourself some other towns to mooch around. Both White Salmon and Hood River are hot spots for the adventurous types – and that makes them very affluent and a bit of a bubble. Go outside those areas and you start to see real America. It’s cool, if a bit deprived.

Enjoy it. It’s a fucking hell of a place to visit and we both feel entirely privileged to have now been to this hot spot so many times. When the riding’s on, it’s world class. And the buzz around this place is electric. AWSI week is obviously a hive of all the pro riders and brand guys, but go to the hatchery carpark on a big day in summer and be prepared to see a bunch of these regular faces riding past you then too.
