As featured in Foiling Magazine · Issue 30 ↗

Although this editor couldn’t make it to the Kernow Foil Classic event (again) for its fourth year, by all accounts – and by chuff there have been many – it was a proper barnstormer. Blessed by near-perfect conditions, and with riders from across the globe showing up, the event’s profile continues to grow, while its reputation as one of the best grassroots foil events on the planet remains perfectly intact. Freddie Oldfield, roving reporter and 50% of the Generic Foiling Podcast, wired in this report…

Words: Freddie Oldfield · Photos: Elliot Glynn & Sam Light

Four years in, and the Kernow Foil Classic (KFC) has morphed into an absolute phenomenon of an event. The highlight of the UK’s foilsports calendar has now fully cemented itself as one of the best events in the world. I’m definitely biased, as a friend of the organizer and a UK industry rep – but I’ll stand by every word of it.

A meeting of minds with the team behind another already successful event, taking place later in October in Crozon, Brittany, France, has enabled organizer Chris Burke and his team to combine resources, creating a real sense of momentum for the European foil scene.

Foilers from all over the UK and now much further afield gathered at Gwithian in Cornwall UK for a whole week of froth – and boy, were they met with some conditions. There was honestly nothing more you could hope for – we had wind when we needed wind, swell when we needed swell, and the hot summer we’d all just had meant we were riding said conditions in 2mm and 3mm wetsuits all week long, which (to be honest!) is unheard of.

Joining forces this year with the Crozon team, along with securing landowner permissions 12 months ago, meant that Chris had a ton of time to prep for the 2026 event and a great team to bounce ideas off. Judges have been shared between the two events this year to create continuity of scoring between the French and British events, and media teams with immense experience have been called in to cover every single wave across the two weeks of events. Martin (Crozon) and Chris (Kernow) also got on like a house on fire – that’s never going to harm the bonding of these two events.

We started the week with firing wing and parawing conditions. With all the international riders managing to get into town early enough, Chris made the call to quietly run his “Flow Show” style parawing event the day before the official start of competition, while we had great wind bumps to run in. An idea stolen entirely from Hugh Mitchell’s similar showcase in Australia – 10 riders would do two runs down the same stretch of coastline, each one filmed by the media team and edited to the rider’s choice of song. The videos would then be combined and shown at the Thursday night social at Gilbert’s pub in Hayle, and the crowd would vote on who they thought used the conditions best to show off their talents.

First place went to Finn Spencer and his usual show of power, with growing UK legend Will Haslam in second place and frother Harvey Thompson in third with his solid riding and my personal favorite song choice (’90s hip-hop always wins). Chris was smart to cap the votes at 10:30pm, as Harvey must have bought the whole bar a round after that – his votes had gone through the roof by midnight.

The Duotone-supported wing comp kicked off the official competition on Wednesday, with shoulder-high waves and a relatively decent wind blowing cross-shore. Gwithian’s always hit or miss on the inside, with the cliffs blocking the wind – but there was just enough to punch over the whitewater on the smaller waves, if timed right! A real showcase was put on by the pro division later in the day as we started whittling the field down through the quarterfinals and beyond. But everyone and their dog got a chance to show what they were made of throughout the day, and there were smiles left, right, and center as most people got washed in at the end of their sessions or heats after a flogging in the whitewater.

The finals were completed on Friday when the wind and swell properly aligned again, with a proper showdown to finish off the men’s pro division. Finn clinched it with a huge last-minute turn in more whitewater than I’d know what to do with, while Cash Berzolla had a rarely seen bailed send. Finn took first, Cash second, and Benoit Carpentier third.

The girls deserve a special highlight this year as the frothiest group of the week. From year one of the KFC with no female entries to year four with 15 – these girls seemed to love every second and really put themselves on the map riding in such powered conditions. Their infectious smiles followed them through each social evening they attended as one big girl gang. Olympic gold medalist in kitefoiling, Ellie Aldridge, and Red Bull’s Queen of the Air Francesca “The Tank” Maini put on a real show in the final, with Cesca pipping Ellie to the post with some really powered turns back into the whitewater in awesome conditions.

Foil assist categories were added this year, and boy, were they a highlight. We’ve never seen riders push foil assist in conditions this size on the world stage before. The initial rounds were run down the bay in Hayle until the tide halted play – at which point Chris made the decision to move back up the road to Gwithian and its notoriously more powerful waves. This decision was met with some serious trepidation from the riders, but judging by the faces as everyone came out of the water at the end of the day, it was the right call yet again from event director and guru, Chris.

Seeing some of the world’s best rip head-high waves apart on their various types of lithium systems was a real sight to behold – and one that will surely garner worldwide attention. I’d go as far as saying Chris and the KFC team have pushed foil assist into a whole new realm – no longer is this thriving discipline relegated to just gentler conditions. Supported by Foil Drive but no longer solely dominated by the brand, the category saw Duotone, Manta, and RIIFT systems joining the fold of competitive riding – a real broadening of horizons in the lithium world. So much so that Duotone riders took all three podium spots – Finn Spencer in his usual top spot, Malae McElheny in second, and Jeffrey Spencer in third.

Come the weekend, the wind backed off and the swell abated a touch – bring in the Armstrong-supported prone game for Saturday. The aim was to smash through these rounds in one day and finish the finals in time for sunset and the awards in the evening before the big party night. This then left Sunday as a relaxed day for freeriding. Prone was the main discipline shared between the Crozon and Kernow events, with the overall winner to be crowned King of the “Celtic Cup” after the second event. Prone foiling is currently the self-proclaimed pinnacle of our sports – like shortboarding in surfing – sitting in the top spot thanks to how difficult it is to master and its simplicity in output.

The prone comp couldn’t have been a better ending to the competition. Great conditions throughout the day, crystal-clear waters, and a beautiful sunset for the finals of each category – culminating in the men’s pro final, which was an absolute face-melter. Ten seconds into said final, all four boys took off on four individual waves and showed the three-dimensional nature of foiling – all firing off in different directions and creating confusion for anyone trying to keep up with the action. It was magic.

Luke Atkinson scored two great waves early in the heat. Finn had a banger and then struggled to get another. Benoit had some great little rights but couldn’t quite get the ones he needed. Jack Ho scored early with that first wave and then started getting cramps in his legs after a week of surfing his face off. All looked lost for him until, right toward the end of the heat, he came screaming out of the gate with one last set wave. Finn then grabbed his wave of the heat just before the flag went down and had an absolute screamer – was it enough to beat Luke’s early scores, though?! This media moguler was on the beach ready to interview the winner as they came off the water… but the results never appeared. That last wave had obviously thrown a spanner in the works between Finn and Luke, and a decision was made on the fly for the judges to deliberate further as the riders came back up to the crowds and the soon-to-be podium on the cliff. Turns out the judges decided to re-watch the entire heat on video before making their decisions – which made for an awesome reveal as the riders took their rightful places atop the podium. Luke first, Finn second, Jack third, and Benoit fourth.

Worthy mentions – this event is still a grassroots event at heart. Chris doesn’t want that to change – and the amateurs and groms haven’t even been mentioned yet in my little writings due to my enforced maximum word count from the editor! Giving these guys a platform to perform on is important for the progression of riding standards within the UK, Europe, and further afield, and without these guys and girls competing, the event wouldn’t have the same atmosphere. The level in all divisions has taken a notable step up, and it was awesome to see everyone competing at such a high standard. The UK riders across all disciplines did us proud, and I’m excited to see what next year brings with that in mind!

Lastly – purely because I feel like they should be able to read their names in these glossy pages – a few folks who have earned kudos for their participation: Sack Jalmon, Will The Haslam, and Ziggy Gorringe for bringing character, enthusiasm, and now international-level riding skills to the event, returning four years in a row and beginning to take the water sports world by storm. Tom Warrell for smashing the amateur wing discipline. Jowan Vittle for being the highest-placed Cornish rider in the whole comp by taking first in the junior prone division. And finally, Holly Pye for being the only female rider who headed out in those meaty conditions in foil assist.

An unbelievably big hats off to Chris and his long-suffering partner, Gig. Yes, there’s a growing team around Chris as each year spirals upward – but there’s still only one family at the top of the food chain, and the efforts they put into this event year after year are not to be scoffed at by anybody. Chris is the one at the helm, and he knows exactly where he wants the ship to point. A man with an idea is dangerous, but a man with an idea, a plan to fulfill it, and four years’ worth of reputation behind him is someone not to be messed with…
