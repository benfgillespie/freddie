Buckle up, this has become a long read.

A discipline of foiling that has taken a firm hold over the last few years – and foil assist isn't going anywhere anytime soon. But which foil assist are you? We may not have ridden the Duotone system, and our Riift experience is limited, but we've got thoughts and opinions on the matter at hand… and you lot keep asking us foil assist questions, so we thought we'd put them out in the world for scrutiny.

We also know there are other systems on the market, but these are the three players in our mind. Other competitors seem to be competing mainly on price – and while this is totally fine – they're structurally the same as what's on the market already, so we're not considering them up there with these three head honchos. Well, there is that Waydoo boost thing but we know nothing about it and it looks a bit odd.

So, the list – the big dogs:

  1. Foildrive — the OG foil assist. They started it all, paved the way and did the legwork to legitimise and grow this market segment.
  2. Riift — in our opinion the first real-world competitor who have addressed some remaining issues not yet ironed out with the 'standard' bottom-mount foil assist system.
  3. Duotone — repping the body-mounted battery system in this list. Yes, there are other brands selling similar-ish systems, but Duotone is the only big dog and probably the one most people leaning towards body-mounted batteries are going to go towards. These are highly tuned pieces of electronics mixing with salt water – most people that chat to us share the same opinion that they want some cash flow behind the supplier of their very expensive bit of salt-water-thrashed electronica, just in case any issues do crop up.

Why is the Lift X not on the list? Well, we're not 110% sure. Largely lack of exposure on our part, but also a quick Google reveals their lightest setup at 20kg/44lb which is still porky compared to the others. That 20kg setup also states a maximum rider weight of 175lb/79kg, which is curious – not sure if that's more of a recommendation than a maximum, but the website said maximum. Who knows! We'll circle back to Lift in the future perhaps.

Remember, lots of the below statements will be opinions – but you all keep asking for ours, so here they are. Enjoy our mix of genuine product comparison insights and cliché caricature stereotypes. If you want to leave your opinion in the comments, please do.

FoilDrive

Who's buying the FoilDrive?

You're already a foiler of some description and you fancy a dabble in this fancy-looking foil assist market. You don't want to skimp, and you keep hearing Foildrive is the best option. You want more time on the water to enjoy yourself, and you want more time on the water to get better at foiling in general. You might want to have a go at downwinding and see this as the only realistic way of doing it in the short term – maybe ever. You don't want to rip up your current foiling setup, you just want it as a bolt-on extra to see what all the fuss is about and whether you're going to like it or not – and you want to buy a product with upgradability in mind.

Freddie's opinions on FoilDrive

Despite the age of this discipline, Foildrive is now seen as the OG, the staple. They've proven themselves worthy of this mantle with their great product and company support. They invented this discipline and currently they still own it. The brand started off chucking a bunch of electronics into a lunchbox and sticky-taping it onto your board, but it's evolved into a very swanky-looking market leader.

It's a little clunky still, and even with integrated masts and trench boards can be a bit of a faff to set up for the uninitiated – but the design stood almost entirely uninterrupted until this year, where we've seen an influx of potential, real-world competitors.

The joy of the Foildrive is the compatibility of it. It'll stick on anything. You can use it on anything. Yes, that's expanded now and there are Foildrive-specific boards and numerous integrated masts – but you don't need to start with all that gubbins. It has been exciting to see how each brand has jumped on the bandwagon out of sheer necessity to keep up with what has become one of the most pivotal parts of our sports. You now don't even need to start with a brand new unit – the introduction of the Fusion and the Max+ systems recently have meant there's now a bit more of a second-hand market floating about and some really good kit at much cheaper prices.

Saying it's the best of a bad bunch is the wrong framing, but there are some obvious design elements of the Foildrive that make it a tad annoying at times – think the battery removal in a trench board for instance, or the lining up of bolts while connecting the system in the first place. While these are certainly not deal-breakers, they do leave gaps open for the likes of the next couple of brands we'll mention to come in and gobble up some market share. We've had the pleasure of meeting and becoming friends with the Foildrive team over the last few years – and I am confident in saying that there's no other team I trust to iron out niggles like these over the coming years, hopefully without pissing off their own clientele base in the long term. Yes, change upsets folks – you can see that with grumbles from customers who bought MAX units just prior to the MAX+ coming out – but in such a quickly-moving discipline you're always going to piss someone off. We know the guys at Foildrive take this into account every time and aren't just bringing stuff out willy-nilly. It's a solid system and they're a solid company – nuff said.

Liam's thoughts on FoilDrive

When the first FoilDrive Assist was released over five years ago, it was the obvious choice because it was the only choice and therefore the best choice. When the Gen 2 Max came out, it cemented FoilDrive as the 'Hoover' of the foil assist market. Only recently has 'foil assist' been a term adopted by the industry to incorporate alternative platforms.

There are now a few base models to choose from: the Fusion, the Max+ and the Slim. I'm going to ignore the Slim in this blog because Slim customers know who they are and they already own a unit. It has been confirmed that the current Slim will be replaced by a Slim+ at some point, just as the Gen 2 Max was replaced by the Max+ – but we're not sure when. In my opinion, if you're not 95% confident you're a Slim customer, you're probably not a Slim customer.

So 90%+ of people are probably Max+ or Fusion customers, and in my opinion the choice is clear. If you're reading this and don't yet own a unit and one of the following is true, you're probably a Fusion customer: runtime/max wave count without battery swaps is your absolute priority; you're a particularly powerful (heavy) rider; or you're a rider with relatively low skill or mobility but still want to ride small gear and will be taking off onto foil in flat water most of the time. If none of those specific cases apply to you, you're likely a Max+ rider. I'm ~80kg and ride a Max+ and it has more than enough juice for a small board and foil and a good runtime. I wouldn't want more runtime at the cost of more weight – I just don't think it's necessary. I'm already doing an hour easy peasy, and if I was mindful to be economical with my takeoffs, I'm sure I could do 1.5 hours without much bother.

What has impressed me about the brand over the past years is the support they've offered customers. I've been a member of the FoilDrive Owner's group on Facebook for years and they really do give a shit. I think it's a credit to them that second-hand units are starting to appear on the market commanding a strong price and being snapped up – especially when you consider how much typical foiling gear has devalued less than 12 months after release. This is electronics being exposed to harsh wave environments in salt water we're talking about. Mental! It also says a lot that almost every foil brand under the sun sells FoilDrive integrated masts as an optional upgrade that most seem to end up with once they've swallowed the pill.

So yeah, I like the company and I like the product. My main critical thoughts: we tried the original Assist back in 2021/2022 and it was just too much of a faff for us to be interested at that time. Gen 2 was a legitimate game changer and Freddie and I both wanted one. But there were still too many small bolts for anyone's liking to attach the motor to the mast and lining up the track bolts was often a pain.

The small bolts are no longer a problem because they aren't required – you now use the universal mini pod which is an excellent piece of design and attaches the motor quicker, easier and in a more streamlined manner than any of the competitors. It hasn't removed the need for tape though. The truth is, most really keen foilers that switch disciplines a lot will aim to have a FoilDrive mast and a non-FoilDrive mast just so they aren't developing RSI from winding and unwinding tape all the time. I've genuinely noticed my use of the product increase since having a dedicated mast, removing the need to swap all the time. The track nut faff has been half-solved by the split bolts. It still isn't as easy as attaching a mast direct to a board, but it requires a lot less fettling than it used to.

Riift

Who's buying the Riift?

If you're buying Riift, you're probably one of the following:

  • You're a good foiler and you want the least possible draggy bits and minimal faff so you can go and lay down mad carves in the swell.
  • You've been playing on foil assist systems since the beginning and just want the latest and greatest.
  • You were swept up in the pre-release hype before you even knew how much it was.
  • You've been keeping an eye on the market for a while and until now, you've just thought it could be done better.
  • You've thought about getting a foil assist before, but there wasn't a brand or product with a cool enough image for you.

Freddie's opinions on the Riift

We thought it might've been the Flite Amp that rocked the foil assist status quo back in 2025, but it wasn't until this year we saw a real-life competitor that seemed to address some of the frustrations with the design of the 'standard' foil assist. It's sleek, it's polished, it's simple, it's streamlined. It's unproven – as of yet. At the time of writing it's October 2026 and it's not out yet, but we know it's at least en route and the guys are working hard behind the scenes.

There are many conversations to have about it if you're interested – is the battery life up to scratch? Are my second and third batteries going to cost me an absolute fortune? Do I have to buy a specific board straight off the bat? All these things will be answered fully over the coming six to twelve months when the system is out in the wild. For now, I keep coming back to the question – if you saw one of each of these setups on the beach, which would you grab and use? To me, right now – I would go Riift out of sheer simplicity and streamlined-ness.

A specific board straight off the bat is going to reduce the amount of newcomers who go for a Riift in my eyes. And for those keen-bean foil-assisters who currently own six batteries for their current system, I don't see them swapping once they see the inevitable price of spare batteries – due to the 'battery box' also housing the whole power delivery system, meaning you're replacing nigh on everything.

Still – it's a hell of a product and it's exciting a lot of folks. I can only imagine it'll be pushing Foildrive's design team even harder to come up with something similar.

Liam's opinions on the Riift

The Riift system is as close to Lift's standard of 'click and go' as any of the foil assist options. It is impressively simple and offers a satisfying drop-in battery which clicks into place in their proprietary recess – which has to be built into the board. At the time of writing the webshop went live two days ago, so we've only just found out the price, and sure enough it is the most expensive system and you have no choice but to buy a Riift-enabled board on top of this.

That said, the fact that so many brands and board builders have been making these dedicated Riift boards during the development and hype-build phase of 2026, and some of foiling's big dogs like Kane De Wilde getting so involved, shows that there is an appetite for even more foil assist development. The system does nip the setup elegance crown even when compared to a FoilDrive paired with most trench/channel boards. The Axis pocket trench and the new KT option would be the closest FoilDrive equivalent in my mind.

One of the interesting things to me is that they've chosen to use a different motor size to others. I believe most brands are using variants of something close to 6384 motors – this refers to the rotor/motor can diameter and the rotor length. The Riift motor has a smaller diameter but is longer. The reduced frontal area should be good for hydrodynamics and reduce drag – assuming the length hasn't increased so much that the total wetted area offsets the reduced frontal area. Both gut feeling prior to use and experience on the water says the drag, when going straight at least, is less.

I've only used the Riift system in as good as flat water – from memory it was with an 800 foil and the 3P battery and that orange and pink Jarrod Snow board you've probably seen on the interwebs. It had more than enough juice to get onto foil in flat water and felt nice motoring around. I went back to back between the Riift setup and a FoilDrive Max+ on a trench board. To me they felt similar, with the Riift offering slightly reduced drag upon dipping the motor pod in the water and a slightly more familiar pumping feel – the pump cadence and feedback felt more similar to my normal pumping, requiring slightly less adaptation to account for the weight. It also felt like I had to adjust my stance between motoring and not motoring a tiny bit less, though I couldn't pin down a solid reason for this.

I liked the Riift system and am really pleased to see another brand being started from scratch by passionate foilers, just as FoilDrive was years ago. They've brought something substantively different and it's on the premium end too. I think it's so much better for the progression of gear and sport for people to focus on making product better instead of competing on price. The fact that it not only feels similar to ride to a FoilDrive but seems to excel in a couple of areas is a serious compliment. Pair that with another step forward in terms of convenience, and you've got a good recipe. The real question for many is: is it worth the extra $$$ on the price tag?

The Duotone Assist

Who's buying the Duotone Assist?

This whole weight-on-your-midriff thing really got you thinking. I was the same. You disagreed with the Foildrive fanboys who said weight on the board must be better. Weight on the body and reducing swing weight on the board seems like a no-brainer in your eyes.

You're a Duotone fanboy. You've already got the Duotone logo on your favourite pants and socks, but now you want it on your foil assist too. Honestly, you'd buy anything these guys made and you thought foil assist was rubbish up until your favourite brand told you it was cool.

Freddie's opinions on the Duotone Assist

Liam and I don't foil assist as often as we parawing/wing/SUP etc., so we really, really notice the weight when we strap lithium to our setups. The Duotone system coming to market really made you think about this. Their team riders continually taking the top podium spots in the (very few) foil assist competitions then gets you wondering if it's the system that's allowing them to do more radical manoeuvres in the waves, or just the fact that they're world class riders. This is what team riders do though – sell kit to folks who think they'll be able to ride like them because of the equipment.

I've only used the system once, and on flat water – so once again these are all opinions. I'm far from a Duotone fanboy, but even I can't put fault on them bringing a good system to market and making it look smart too. I don't like the looks of the trigger finger system – I see the niceties of not having to hold onto a trigger when you're actually on the wave, but out of sheer complexity I wonder if it was worth it, changing the trigger system to some weird watch thing.

Last opinion I heard from someone who'd used both Foildrive and the Duotone Foil Assist: the Foildrive was much nicer to motor around on, but the Duotone system felt better on the wave.

Liam's opinions on the Duotone Foil Assist

I've never used one or had a particularly close look at them to be honest. I'm pretty sure I'm set on the idea of battery weight being removed from the board to make the setup feel more like my daily driver that I use for winging and parawinging – but have yet to have a go to confirm whether it does in fact feel that way. I'm not convinced by those that say it is definitely better to have weight on the board. There are too many riders that are better than me with more mass on their torso and I know if someone gave me a bag of sugar to take on a downwind run, I'd stuff it in my wetsuit before I strapped it to my board.

That said, I don't like the idea of parts of the foil assist system being strapped to my body. The reason for that isn't just the one you're probably thinking of. I've been around lithium batteries that have suffered far more abuse than foil assist systems in my FPV drone days, which seems to have desensitised me to the stories you hear about battery bank/e-scooter lithium fires. I just have this irrational distaste for the extra faff and the dangly leash/cord system. I like that with the more traditional foil assist systems, your entire setup is contained within the board.

I'm also not convinced that the runtime would be enough for me. The combined capacity of the battery set (two separate batteries) that you insert into the belt is about half of the new Max+ battery. It would not surprise me if they release a belt in the next 12 months allowing you to use three batteries instead of two – that might change things for me.

I'm curious about the controller system as well. Do you really need a watch and a thumb/finger probe? I don't know, I haven't tried it! I was worried about the durability of it when I first saw it at 2025 AWSI, but I haven't heard about any issues – and the Duotone team absolutely crushed it in the foil assist division of the Kernow Foil Classic this year in the heaviest conditions I've ever witnessed a foil assist in, so that's a pretty good litmus test.

So I'll circle back to where I started – I haven't used it. Maybe if I did, I'd experience a new level of foil assist agility and fall in love with it. Maybe I'll find out one day!
