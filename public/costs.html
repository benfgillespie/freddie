What changes, what it costs, and how the shop and payments would work if you switch genericfoilingpod.com to the new site.

Prices checked 21 October 2026. Card fees are the providers’ standard UK rates. Rough conversions use $1 ≈ £0.78.

The short version

  • Hosting goes from “something you pay Hostinger” to £0. The site runs on Ben’s Vercel account, which he already pays for, and the GFP site fits comfortably inside it.
  • The domain, podcast hosting and newsletter don’t change.
  • For the shop, we recommend Stripe Payment Links: no monthly fee, about 2.3% on a typical tee order, and checkout with Apple Pay and Google Pay.
  • Avoid Snipcart (what the new site is wired for at the moment) unless you sell more than about £780 of merch every month. Below that it’s a flat ~£16/month on top of card fees.
  • ⚠️ The domain expires on 23 November 2026. Renew it whatever you decide.

Why there’s nothing to “run”

WordPress is a program running on a server: it needs updates, plugin renewals and security patches, and it slows down as plugins pile up. The new site is just ready-made pages. There’s nothing to update or hack, and it loads much faster.

The shop & taking payments

You sell four items (three tees and the beanie) at £12–25. At that size, the big question is whether a shop tool’s monthly fee is worth paying. Here are the realistic options:

Option A: Stripe Payment Links Recommended

  • Each product gets a Buy button that opens Stripe’s own secure checkout page, with dropdowns for size and colour.
  • Customers pay by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay. Stripe collects their delivery address and emails them a receipt.
  • You see each order in the Stripe app (with the size, colour and address) and post it as you do now. Money lands in your bank account, usually within a few days.
  • Cost: no monthly fee. 1.5% + 20p per sale on standard UK cards (2.5% + 20p on European cards, 3.15% + 20p on other international cards).
  • Downside: no basket. Someone wanting a tee and a beanie checks out twice, or you add a ready-made “Tee + Beanie bundle” link.

Option B: Snipcart + Stripe (what the new site is built with now)

  • A proper basket that slides out on the site, so customers can buy several things in one checkout.
  • Cost: Snipcart charges $20/month (~£16) whenever sales are under $1,000 (~£780) a month, and 2% of sales above that. That’s on top of Stripe’s 1.5% + 20p.
  • Billed in US dollars, so the cost moves with the exchange rate.

Option C: Shopify Starter

  • About £5/month. You set the products up in Shopify and drop “Buy” buttons onto the site. It handles stock and shipping labels, which is handy if merch grows.
  • Card fees on top. Check Shopify’s UK page for the current Starter rate before choosing this.

Other payment details

  • Whose account? The Stripe account should be in the podcast’s name (Freddie and/or Liam), paying into your bank account, not Ben’s. Setting it up takes about 15 minutes, with ID and bank details.
  • PayPal: Stripe’s checkout covers cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, which is most people. If you want PayPal as well, it can be added, but it costs more (2.9% + 30p for UK PayPal payments).
  • Disputes (someone tells their bank “I didn’t get it”) cost £20 each with Stripe, so keep proof of postage.
  • Old orders: export your WooCommerce order and customer history before the WordPress site is switched off.

What the switch involves

  1. Renew the domain at WordPress.com before 23 November 2026.
  2. Set up Stripe in the podcast’s name and create a Payment Link for each product (Claude can walk you through it). Then we swap the site’s shop buttons over.
  3. Switch on the contact form so messages reach the GFP Gmail.
  4. Redirect old addresses so existing links keep working: /about-us → /about, /episodes-list → /episodes, and the old episode pages → the episodes list.
  5. Point the domain at Vercel. This means changing two settings in WordPress.com’s domain panel. Ben does the Vercel side. The site switches over within an hour or so, with no downtime.
  6. Check everything, then export your WooCommerce orders and cancel Hostinger (and any plugin subscriptions).

Good to know: the site’s files live on GitHub and its hosting is on Ben’s account. If you ever want it fully separate from Ben, it can move to your own GitHub account and your own hosting in an afternoon. Cloudflare Pages and Netlify both have free plans that allow a shop. The domain, Stripe, Mailchimp and the podcast feed are all yours already.
